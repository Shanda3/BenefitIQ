## dental-exclusion

Medicare does not pay for the care, treatment, filling, removal, or replacement of teeth, or of the structures that support the teeth. Those structures are the gums, the junction of the gum and the tooth, the periodontal membrane, the cementum, and the alveolar bone, including the tooth sockets.

## not-covered-examples

Routine dental care is not paid. Taking out an impacted tooth is not paid. Dental work done to prepare the mouth for dentures is not paid. That preparation includes reshaping the alveolar ridge, rebuilding a dental ridge, a frenectomy, and removing a bony growth on the roof of the mouth, when the work is for the dentures.

## jaw-fracture

Stabilizing or immobilizing teeth in connection with reducing a jaw fracture is an example Medicare can pay.

## jaw-splint

A dental splint is an example Medicare can pay only when it is used as part of covered treatment of a covered medical condition, such as a dislocated jaw joint.

## ridge-tumor

Rebuilding a dental ridge is an example Medicare can pay when the rebuild is done because a tumor was removed, and at the same time as that surgery.

## ancillary-xray

When the dental service itself is covered, related services can be paid as well. The page names anesthesia, diagnostic x-rays, and use of an operating room. It does not name a cone-beam code number.

## coordination

For a dental service to be treated as linked to a covered medical service, the medical and dental providers have to coordinate the care, and that coordination has to be in the record. Without that record, Medicare does not pay the dental service.

## enrolled

The provider who bills Medicare for a covered dental service has to be enrolled in Medicare.
